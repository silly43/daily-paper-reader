Abstract 9

Nanopore direct RNA sequencing enables direct profiling of RNA modifications on native transcripts, but accurate 10 

multi-modification detection remains limited by non-stationary signals and heterogeneity across chemistries. Here, 11 

we develop WattmaMod, a deep learning framework for multi-modification detection from nanopore direct RNA se-12 

quencing data. It combines self-supervised pretraining, supervised contrastive fine-tuning, and low-label incremental 13 

adaptation to improve representation learning and support e ffi cient extension to low-resource modification types. The 14 

framework further incorporates wavelet-guided multi-scale encoding and dynamic cross-attention fusion to model 15 

raw signals and event-level features. Results show that WattmaMod achieves robust detection of multiple RNA mod-16 

ifications, including m6A, m5C, m1A, A-to-I, m7G, hm5C, m1 Ψ, f5C, ac4C, m5U and Ψ. It also extends e ffi ciently 17 

to low-resource modification types with minimal labeled data, generalizes across sequencing chemistries and species, 18 

and predicts potential higher-order local organization among distinct RNA modifications. WattmaMod thus provides 19 

a scalable framework for high-resolution epitranscriptome profiling and expands RNA modification analysis beyond 20 

single-site prediction to coordinated multi-modification characterization. 21

to cancer initiation and progression 7–9 . However, it remains challenging to systematically profile the distribution and 23 

crosstalk of multiple modifications on native RNA molecules 10–12 . Conventional techniques, including MeRIP-seq 13 ,24 

miCLIP 14 , m6ACE-Seq 15 , GLORI 16 rely on antibody enrichment or chemical conversion and typically require RNA 25 

fragmentation, thereby disrupting long-range transcript information and molecular heterogeneity. Nanopore direct 26 

RNA sequencing (DRS) enables real-time, amplification-free sequencing of native RNA 17 , o ff ering the potential to 27 

profile multiple modifications on single molecules 18–20 . Quantitative deconvolution of distinct modifications from raw 28 

current signals remains a bottleneck for broader nanopore DRS applications 21,22 .29 

Computational methods for nanopore DRS modification detection largely fall into two paradigms. Compara-30 

tive statistical tools, including xPore 23 , DRUMMER 24 and Nanocompore 25 , identify candidate sites by comparing 31 

signal distributions between conditions, but their performance depends on well-matched designs and high coverage, 32 

limiting single-sample analyses and robustness to batch e ff ects. In parallel, supervised deep-learning models such 33 

as m6Anet 26 , DENA 27 and SingleMod 28 enable single-nucleotide prediction for m6A, motivating extensions toward 34 

multi-modification detection. However, existing multi-modification models, such as TandemMod 29 , CHEUI 30 , Di-35 

rectRM 31 , and the recently proposed ORCA 32 , still face practical challenges in cross-chemistry generalization and 36 

in high-precision extension to novel modification types. Notably, although ORCA supports multi-modification detec-37 

tion, further improvement is still needed in its detection accuracy and extensibility to additional modification types. 38 

These limitations underscore the need for a unified framework supporting multiple chemistries, extensible to new 39 

modification types and robust across domains. 40 

To detect RNA modifications, deep learning models must move beyond designed event features to extract robust 41 

multiscale representations directly from raw signals, thereby decoupling subtle modification-induced perturbations 42 

from background noise 33–36 . The architecture must remain extensible across nanopore chemistries and invariant to 43 

sequence context, enabling e ffi cient adaptation to new modification types or experimental settings 37 . While RNA004 44 

delivers superior signal quality, the paucity of curated, labeled training data for this new iteration poses a substantial 45 

bottleneck to e ff ective model optimization. Furthermore, the framework should generate multi-granular quantitative 46 

outputs at both single-molecule and site-specific levels, facilitating the robust synthesis of per-read probabilities into 47 

site-wise modification scores. 48

Here we present WattmaMod, a label-e ffi cient and lightweight framework for single-molecule nanopore DRS 49 

analysis that integrates wavelet-based multi-scale decomposition with contrastive pretraining and task-specific fine-50 

tuning. By combining self-supervised representation learning on large-scale unlabeled signals with low-label super-51 

vised adaptation, WattmaMod is designed to capture transferable modification-associated patterns under limited anno-52 

tation. To test its robustness and scalability for multi-modification detection, we systematically evaluated WattmaMod 53 

across RNA002 and RNA004 chemistries and diverse modification types. Specifically, under minimal labeled data 54 

conditions, WattmaMod achieves strong discriminative ability with an average AUC of 0.95 across seven RNA modifi-55 

cation types, outperforming existing methods by an average of 10% and by up to 30% for specific types, demonstrating 56 

robust ranking performance even in low-resource settings. Further analyses in human cell lines, perturbation systems 57 

and cross-species settings suggested model-inferred potential co-occurrence patterns among RNA modifications, pro-58 

viding hypotheses for future experimental validation of coordinated RNA modification organization. These results 59 

establish WattmaMod as a scalable framework for accurate and extensible RNA modification profiling from nanopore 60 

DRS data. 61 

## Results 62 

Overview of WattmaMod 63 

To achieve accurate RNA modification detection from nanopore direct RNA sequencing (DRS) for both RNA002 and 64 

RNA004 chemistries, we developed WattmaMod, a unified learning framework that jointly models ionic current-level 65 

and event-level evidence. WattmaMod integrates raw current traces with event-level statistics—including mean, stan-66 

dard deviation, median, and dwell time—together with base-call quality scores and local sequence context to form a 67 

complementary representation for each candidate nucleotide site (Fig. 1a). This design directly targets two practical 68 

bottlenecks in DRS-based RNA modification detection: the often subtle modification-induced current perturbations 69 

and the long-range dependencies that are di ffi cult to capture with single-scale signal representations. By coupling 70 

multi-scale signal encoding with sequence-context modeling, WattmaMod increases the separability of modified ver-71 

sus unmodified signals and improves robustness across datasets and chemistries (Fig. 1b,d). To further address limited 72 

supervision in many settings, we adopt a contrastive learning–based training strategy that combines self-supervised 73 

pretraining with supervised fine-tuning. This scheme yields more stable optimization and stronger generalization in 74

Fig. 1 | Overview of WattmaMod and data processing workflow. a Data preprocessing pipeline. Raw FAST5 and POD5 files are first basecalled to generate FASTQ sequences and aligned to the reference genome. Resquiggle /alignment is applied to obtain calibrated current signals and event-level features, including base identity, mean, standard deviation, median, dwell time, and base-call quality. b Schematic of the WattmaMod model architecture. The inputs, including normalized current signals and event descriptors, are processed by the WSCN and BiLSTM modules, fused via a gated cross-attention mechanism, and passed to a Mamba-based encoder for deep representation modeling. A projection head produces final embeddings for both pretraining and fine-tuning. c Contrastive learning losses. Self-supervised contrastive learning constructs positive and negative pairs through augmentation, while supervised contrastive learning leverages label information to promote intra-class compactness and inter-class separation. d Three-stage training strategy. Stage 1: Self-supervised pre-training on unlabeled data. Stage 2: joint full fine-tuning on abundant modifications from both RNA002 and RNA004 chemistries (RNA002: m6A, m5C, m1A; RNA004: m6A, m5C, Ψ). Stage 3: partial fine-tuning with encoder layers frozen for rare modification types (RNA002: inosine, Ψ, hm5C, m7G, f5C, m1 Ψ, m5U; RNA004: m5U, m1Y, hm5C, ac4C).

To determine the optimal training configuration for RNA002, we benchmarked high-confidence in vitro resources, 76 

including the Curlcake synthetic sequence dataset 38,39 , the ELIGOS in vitro transcription (IVT) dataset 40 , and the in 77

models trained on IVT reached ROC–AUC = 0.987 on matched data but dropped to 0.829 on IVET (Supplementary 80

fore selected IVET with 5-mers as the default RNA002 configuration. For RNA004, the same 5-mer configuration 82 

was adopted considering the model architecture and training strategy, and Curlcake in vitro transcripts were further 83 

incorporated to provide higher-confidence supervision and test extensibility to the RNA004 chemistry 41 . These re-84 

sults establish the training configuration used for subsequent WattmaMod evaluation and support its capacity to learn 85 

transferable modification-associated signals. 86 

Interpretable signal–feature remodeling driven by RNA modifications 87 

To define the signal signatures captured by WattmaMod for modification discrimination, we systematically com-88 

pared paired modified and unmodified reads from the IVT benchmark by jointly analyzing raw ionic current profiles, 89 

event-level statistics and feature attribution patterns. Across motifs, including the m6A-associated ACACU and the 90 

m5C-associated AACAG, modified reads were consistently separated from their unmodified counterparts in ionic cur-91 

rent space, showing clear shifts in mean current and altered fluctuation patterns (Fig. 2a,b; Supplementary Fig. 6g). 92 

This perturbation extended beyond raw signals, modified reads exhibited reduced base-calling quality across multiple 93 

modification types, including m6A, m1A, m5C, hm5C, Ψ, m7G and inosine (Fig. 2c). At the event level, modification 94 

states were further associated with substantial reorganization of feature relationships, including distinct mean-variance, 95 

dwell-time and intensity coupling patterns under m5C and m6A, as well as modification-specific changes in variance 96 

and dwell time in A-centered contexts (Fig. 2d,f; Supplementary Fig. 2a). Feature attribution identified sequence 97 

context and base-calling quality as dominant predictors, with dwell time and signal mean providing additional sup-98 

port (Fig. 2e; Supplementary Fig. 2b). Because modified and unmodified IVT reads were generated from matched 99 

templates, the high attribution of sequence context is not primarily driven by sequence-composition imbalance, but 100

WattmaMod generalizes robustly across independent transcriptomes 105 

WattmaMod generalized robustly across independent datasets and was further strengthened by interval-based dual-106 

threshold confidence filtering under heterogeneous signal distributions 30,42 . To test this, we evaluated the model 107 

on a non-overlapping IVET transcriptome. On the external IVET set, WattmaMod achieved ROC–AUC /PR–AUC 108 

values of 0.956 /0.953 for m6A, 0.972 /0.971 for m5C and 0.885 /0.894 for m1A (Fig. 3a-c), improving m6A and 109 

m5C detection by approximately 2 percentage points over models trained under the same setting. F1 scores also 110 

remained consistently high across datasets under the default threshold (Supplementary Fig. 5a-e; Supplementary 111 

Fig. 6h). When dual-threshold confidence filtering was applied, performance further increased to 0.981 /0.985 for 112 

m6A, 0.992 /0.993 for m5C and 0.955 /0.965 for m1A, while retaining approximately 75%, 81% and 63% of reads, 113 

respectively, at upper /lower thresholds of 0.9 /0.3 (Fig. 3f). This gain was consistent with the clear bimodal separation 114 

of predicted probabilities between modified and unmodified reads (Fig. 3e). Read-length stratification further showed 115 

that performance increased from ∼0.87-0.89 in the 0-200 nt group to ∼0.93-0.97 for reads longer than 600 nt (Fig. 3d; 116 

Supplementary Fig. 5f), indicating stable behavior across read-length regimes. Compared with classical statistical and 117 

conventional machine-learning baselines, including logistic regression, GaussianNB, decision trees, random forests, 118 

KNN, SVM, XGBoost and multilayer perceptron, WattmaMod achieved the highest accuracy for both m5C (0.965) 119 

and m6A (0.913) (Supplementary Fig. 6i-j). 120

We next investigated whether the multi-stage training strategy enables WattmaMod to achieve accurate low-label ex-134 

pansion across diverse RNA modifications and nanopore sequencing chemistries, including RNA002 and RNA004. To 135 

do this, we evaluated the model on RNA002 and RNA004 using only 3,000 positive and 3,000 negative samples per 136 

modification for fine-tuning. Under RNA002, WattmaMod achieved strong performance across multiple modification 137 

types, with PR-AUC values of 0.968 for f5C, 0.929 for hm5C, 0.998 for inosine, 0.946 for m1A, 0.921 for m5U, 138 

0.968 for m7G and 0.989 for Ψ, while also maintaining similarly strong performance for m5U and m1 Ψ in Curlcake 139 

(Fig. 5a). Lift analysis further showed that the top-ranked 10-30% of read segments recovered most true sites (Sup-140 

plementary Fig. 8), indicating e ff ective prioritization even with minimal labeled data. We further evaluated whether 141 

this multi-modification capability could be retained under RNA004. In this setting, modified and unmodified reads 142 

remained clearly separable, F1 scores exceeded 0.94 across most coverage strata (Fig. 5b; Supplementary Fig. 9), 143 

and false positive rates remained low across modification types in both RNA002 (0.042–0.127) and RNA004 (0.038– 144 

0.114) (Supplementary Fig. 20a). Dorado RNA004 modified-base calling was further included as a reference on the 145 

Curlcake synthetic data, using matched modified and unmodified samples to generate PR curves for m6A, m5C and Ψ146 

(Supplementary Fig. 20c). After fine-tuning on the RNA-modbase IVT dataset to adapt to the shorter e ff ective signal 147 

context of RNA004, WattmaMod achieved ROC-AUC values of 0.983-0.999 and PR-AUC values of 0.976-0.999 for 148 

m6A, inosine, Ψ and m5C (Supplementary Fig. 12b,d), indicating that the model is readily transferable beyond a 149 

single sequencing chemistry. The benefit of multi-stage training was further supported by training analyses. Fine-150 

tuning with 3,000 samples yielded the best overall performance (Supplementary Fig. 19), and pretraining modestly 151

## Discussion 206 

To address major challenges in DRS-based modification detection—including limited high-confidence labels, hetero-207 

geneous data quality, the di ffi culty of jointly profiling multiple modification types, and limited interpretability—we 208 

present WattmaMod, a deep-learning framework for RNA modification detection. With supervised fine-tuning on only 209 

250,000 labeled single-molecule reads, WattmaMod outperforms widely used methods in both detection accuracy and 210 

inference throughput, while substantially accelerating GPU execution and reducing computational cost. Owing to 211 

its label-e ffi cient and few-shot adaptability, WattmaMod maintains stable cross-dataset generalization and supports 212 

diverse modification types. 213 

WattmaMod adopts multiscale signal encoding guided by discrete wavelet transform (DWT), where low-frequency 214 

components capture background and steady-state structures and high-frequency components emphasize transient per-215 

turbations introduced by modified bases, enabling selective extraction of modification-related cues across frequency 216 

bands. Dynamic cross-attention further leverages event-derived priors to adaptively reweight multi-scale channels. 217 

In addition, the state-space module integrates long-range context, allowing signal and sequence features to form a 218 

context-aware joint representation. Training follows a two-stage contrastive paradigm: self-supervised pretraining 219 

constructs time–frequency–enhanced positive /negative pairs to obtain noise-robust embeddings, followed by super-220 

vised contrastive fine-tuning with limited labels, where amplitude and temporal perturbations improve class separa-221 

tion and generalization. Together, band decomposition, prior-guided adaptive modulation, SSM-enabled long-range 222 

dependency modeling, and self-supervised initialization contribute to improved robustness, generalization and overall 223 

detection performance relative to baseline approaches. 224 

Across multiple in vitro benchmarks, WattmaMod shows improved detection performance across diverse modifi-225 

cation types compared with existing tools. It further demonstrates stable cross-species generalization in human tumour 226 

cell lines, poplar, Arabidopsis and Saccharomyces cerevisiae . The model enables high-precision single-molecule de-227

It is made The copyright holder for this preprint (which this version posted July 2, 2026. ;https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.02.735990 doi: bioRxiv preprint

tection and reliably captures changes in modification levels across genetic perturbation backgrounds, indicating that 228 

WattmaMod learns transferable biophysical signal patterns rather than dataset-specific noise. 229 

Despite these strengths, several limitations remain. First, some training and evaluation resources—such as Curl-230 

cake long transcripts generated under RNA004 chemistry and RNA-modbase synthetic oligonucleotides with BED-231 

defined modification sites—deviate from native transcriptomes in biological context, structural complexity and read-232 

length distributions. Combined with reduced signal-to-noise near fragment ends and less stable event alignment, these 233 

factors partially impair performance on short reads. In practice, longer reads should be prioritized during inference, to-234 

gether with stringent coverage filtering to obtain higher-confidence sites. Second, model performance depends strongly 235 

on training representativeness. IVET-trained models show markedly improved cross-species and cross-platform gen-236 

eralization compared with IVT-trained counterparts, suggesting that diversity of modification contexts and sequence 237 

environments is often more important than simply scaling dataset size. Third, the current model does not explicitly 238 

incorporate secondary-structure priors during inference, which is known to modulate modification accessibility and 239 

signal manifestation, particularly for structure-sensitive marks. Incorporating structure-aware features (for example, 240 

pairing probability, accessibility, or loop /stem annotations derived from RNAfold-like predictors) may further improve 241 

site resolution and robustness in complex transcriptomic contexts. Future work will incorporate broader species and 242 

tissue sources to build a more neutral and representative foundation dataset, while integrating secondary-structure 243 

priors to mitigate synthetic bias and enhance biological fidelity when interpreting real samples. 244 

WattmaMod also provides a scalable basis for a unified multi-task framework. Such a framework could enable 245 

parallel detection of dozens of modifications within a single model, while integrating spatiotemporal visualization and 246 

rule extraction to characterize competitive and cooperative relationships and further improve interpretability. From 247 

an application perspective, WattmaMod allows rapid adaptation to specific sample types or target marks using only a 248 

small set of validated sites, enabling e ffi cient high-throughput inference for identifying disease-associated or condition-249 

specific RNA modification programs. 250

## Methods 251 

Data and preprocessing 252 

For direct RNA sequencing (DRS) data generated with the RNA002 kit, reads were basecalled using Guppy v6.1.5 253 

with the RNA high-accuracy model. Multi-read FAST5 files were then converted to single-read FAST5 format using 254 

ont fast5 api v4.0.0. The basecalled reads were aligned to the reference transcriptome using minimap2 v2.22 48 . Raw 255 

current signals were subsequently processed with the resquiggle procedure in Tombo v1.5.1 49 to obtain event-level 256 

segmentation and accurate signal-to-reference alignments. 257 

For RNA004 chemistry, POD5 files were basecalled using Dorado v0.9.6 50 . Raw current signals and sequences 258 

were then jointly aligned to the reference transcriptome using Uncalled4 v4.1.0 51 , producing BAM files annotated 259 

with signal index tags (ul and ur). On this basis, we implemented custom scripts to extract raw signal values at single-260 

nucleotide resolution by combining BAM alignments with signal index tags. When the alignment model produced 261 

invalid signal indices (e.g. ul = 0), we applied a forward-filling strategy that replaces them with the nearest preceding 262 

valid index, thereby preserving continuity of the signal–base mapping without introducing substantial bias. Insertions 263 

and deletions in the CIGAR string were handled by inserting gap characters (“-”) into the reference or read sequences, 264 

respectively, and padding the associated signal segments to maintain a one-to-one positional correspondence between 265 

aligned bases and signal samples. Command-line options for these steps are in Supplementary Table 1. 266 

During signal feature construction, we normalized the raw current traces to reduce systematic bias, segmented 267 

signals using Tombo or Uncalled4, and spline-interpolated each segment to a fixed length of 500 samples. For each 268 

event, we computed the mean, standard deviation, median, dwell time, and base-call quality. We then used a 5-mer 269 

window centered on the target nucleotide to aggregate the event-level statistics with the normalized signal features, 270 

forming the input to the deep learning model(Fig. 1a). 271 

Training strategy for models 272 

Direct end-to-end supervised training is prone to overfitting when confronted with multiple RNA modification types 273 

and sparse annotations, which in turn limits the quality of learned representations. We adopt a multi-stage training 274 

strategy. We first perform self-supervised pretraining on a blended RNA002 dataset using only unlabeled, read-level 275

After obtaining the general encoder, the second stage conducts supervised fine-tuning on labeled read-level data 278

full-parameter supervised fine-tuning across the primary modifications in both chemical systems (RNA002: m6A, 280 

m5C, m1A; RNA004: m6A, m5C, Ψ). This stage-wise grouping was defined mainly according to the availability, 281 

scale, and reliability of high-confidence labeled training data for each modification type under each nanopore chem-282 

istry, with biological abundance considered as an additional factor influencing data accessibility. 283 

The third stage targets low-resource and hard-to-obtain modification types by unfreezing the terminal projection 284 

layer and classification head while keeping the backbone encoder parameters unchanged, enabling rapid incremental 285 

adaptation for each new classification task. RNA002 focuses on Inosine (A-to-I), Ψ, hm5C, m7G, f5C, m1 Ψ, and 286 

m5U; RNA004 focuses on m5U, m1 Ψ, hm5C, and ac4C. 287

Model hyperparameter settings and evaluation 372 

During the model training process, we used the AdamW 53 optimizer, with an initial learning rate of 0.001 and a 373 

batch size of 512. A dynamic learning rate adjustment strategy (ReduceLROnPlateau) was employed, which reduces 374 

the learning rate by a factor of 0.1 when the validation metric plateaued for 3 consecutive epochs, with a minimum 375 

learning rate of 1 ×10 −6. To prevent overfitting, early stopping was enabled to terminate training if no improvement was 376 

observed over a certain period. All training was conducted on an NVIDIA A10 GPU. Detailed training hyperparameter 377 

settings are summarized in Supplementary Table 3. 378 

We uniformly split each dataset into training and testing sets at a 4:1 ratio. RNA002 undergoes cross-training 379 

and validation between IVET, Curlcake (RNA002), and IVT. The RNA004 dataset is based on Curlcake (RNA004) 380 

(long sequences) and the rna-modbase in vitro transcription dataset constructed from BED tags (short sequences). The 381
