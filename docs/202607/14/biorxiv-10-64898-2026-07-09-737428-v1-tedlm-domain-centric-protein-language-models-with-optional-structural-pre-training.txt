## Abstract 19 

Conventional protein language models are pretrained on full -length sequences that interleave multiple 20 

domains with linkers and disordered regions, diluting fold -specific signal s. Our approach pretrains 21 

masked language models on structurally -defined domain segments from The Encyclopedia of Domains. 22 

TEDlm learns from domain sequences alone with a standard MLM objective, while its variant TEDlm3D 23 

adds a Cα distance -guided contact los s that supervises the attention maps. On CATH S40 remote -24 

homology detection (<40% identity), the domain -centric pretraining has a bigger effect than model scale: 25 

at the final layer, a 650M -parameter TEDlm achieves an AUROC1 of 0.28 compared to 0.22 for ESM 226 

3B, whereas TEDlm3D reaches 0.50, approaching the structure -based search tool Foldseek (0.53) from 27 

sequence alone at inference. Attention -map and categorical Jacobian probes show that the contact signal 28 

is encoded in the model representations themselves, not only in a trained output head. TEDlm variants 29 

also substantially improve zero -shot Molecular Function prediction over ESM2, while matching it on 30 

various biophysical property tasks, indicating that signals are largely domain -intrinsic. Together, these 31 

results position domain -centric pretraining as a route to compact, structurally informed protein language 32 

models. 33

and HHblits have long been used for this task 4–6. Recent algo rithms like MMseqs2 significantly improved 40

## Discussion 213 

In this work, we introduced TEDlm and TEDlm3D, protein language models pretrained on structurally 214 

defined domain segments from The Encyclopedia of Domains (TED) 29 . Between TEDlm and ESM2, we 215 

have demonstrated the training dataset effect: restricting training to domain -level sequences alone 216 

produces representations that outperform models up to 4 -fold larger on a stringent homology detection 217

another limitation could arise from the fact that pretraining data is drawn from AFDB version 4 3,271 

inh erently biasing toward high -confidence predictions and under -representing rare folds and intrinsically 272 

disordered regions. TEDlm does not incorporate multiple sequence alignments, foregoing the explicit 273 

evolutionary depth that has proven valuable in method s such as AlphaFold2 1,2 . Finally, the performance 274 

gains from intermediate embeddings, documented in the extended data, suggest that final transformer 275 

layers in pLMs are partially optimised for token -level prediction at the expense of global structural 276 

re presentations 30 , a phenomenon warranting further investigation. Future work should explore domain -277 

level deduplication strategies, hierarchical representations bridging domain and protein levels for 278 

functional annotation, and integration of TEDlm3D's doma in -centric approach with multi -domain and 279 

complex -level context. 280

## Contact prediction from single -sequence Jacobians. 355 

We assessed the capacity of each model to encode residue –residue contact information using the 356 

categorical Jacobian approach of Zhang et al 25 . For a protein of length L, we constructed a full (L × 20 × 357 

L × 20) Jacobian tensor by substituting each position i with all 20 canonical amino acids and recording 358 

the change in predicted logits at every other position j relative to the wild -type sequence. The resulting 359 

tensor was centred by iteratively subtracting the mean along each of its four axes, collapsed to an (L × L) 360 

coupling matrix via the Frobenius norm over the two amino -acid dimensions, symmetrized, and corrected 361 

for phylogenetic and positional biases with the average product correction (APC) 43 . For TEDlm3D 362 

models, we additionally evaluated a supervised contact head: a single forward pass through the model 363

## Per -head attention precision 374 

TEDlm and TEDlm3D use Flash Attention, which does not expose attention weights. For each sequence 375 

we registered a forward pre -hook on every attention layer's inner kernel to capture the post -rotary query 376 

and key tensors and recomputed the attention matrix explicitly as scaled -dot -product -attention (SDPA )377 

via softmax(QK ᵀ/√d) per head. Specifically, for both 650M models: 28 layers × 24 heads = 672 heads. To 378 

ensure the attention matrix remain ed the same as Flash Attention, we recomputed weights and the 379 

maximum absolute error < 5×10⁻³ across both models. Each head's L×L attention matrix was symmetrised 380 

(A + A ᵀ) and APC -corrected, then scored by precision against the gold labels. On ly the best performing 381 

head was selected, i.e. the head with the highest mean long -range P@L over the 1,327 CATH S40 T-level 382 

representatives. For TEDlm: layer 27/head 5; and for TEDlm3D: layer 25/head 15, the Figure 2A reports 383
