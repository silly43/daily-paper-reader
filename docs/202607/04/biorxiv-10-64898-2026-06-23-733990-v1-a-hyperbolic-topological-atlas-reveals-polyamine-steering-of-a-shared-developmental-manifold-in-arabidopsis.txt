Abstract 21 

High-throughput plant phenotyping captures development at scale, yet image-rich screens are 22 still often reduced to static trait summaries. We tested whether nutrient availability, polyamine 23 priming, concentration, and their transport reshape Arabidopsis rosette development by 24 generating distinct morphologies or by changing residence along a common trajectory. We 25 analyzed 138,223 time-resolved rosette images from Col-0 and five mutants involved in 26 polyamine transport ( put1-5 ) primed to putrescine, spermidine, spermine, dose, and nutrient 27 regimes using a self-supervised vision backbone, Poincaré embedding, hyperbolic Mapper, and 28 manifold straightening. The data form a single connected developmental manifold with 410 29 nodes and 746 edges, organized from an early, low-nutrient-biased hub through high-30 betweenness transition corridors to two late, nutrient-enriched terminal regions. Polyamine 31 identity stratifies this manifold by developmental phase: putrescine enriches early states, 32 spermidine occupies transition corridors, and spermine marks late compact rosettes. Nutrient 33 richness and dose change distal occupancy, whereas put genotypes alter dwell time within 34 shared regions rather than producing separate topologies. Manifold straightening resolves these 35 effects into a short early lateral deflection followed by convergence, yielding two scalar 36 readouts, early transverse offset and distal occupancy, that summarize treatment action on a 37 common morphodynamic scale. The framework converts large image screens into interpretable 38 developmental geometry for image-based phenomics. 39

Results 99 

A connected Mapper graph orders rosettes by developmental progression 100 

Hyperbolic Mapper reconstruction generated a single connected graph of 410 nodes and 746 101 edges from 138,223 rosette images (Figure 2A-1). Coloring nodes by imaging round revealed 102 a clear developmental gradient: early samples were concentrated in a compact hub, 103 intermediate nodes formed narrow connector regions, and late samples occupied two distal 104 arms. Representative rosette images embedded in the nodes supported this ordering, 105 progressing from small, open seedlings to dense, overlapping canopies. Thus, the dominant 106 structure recovered from images was not a set of isolated treatment clusters, but a connected 107 developmental manifold with alternative late regions. 108 

Polyamine identity maps onto developmental position 109 

Projecting priming metadata onto the same graph revealed phase-specific enrichment without 110 breaking graph connectivity (Figure 2A-3). The effect of putrescine (Put) was concentrated in 111 the early hub, spermidine (Spd) was most frequent in connector nodes, and Spm was enriched 112 at the distal arms, regardless of the used concentration. This distribution is consistent with a 113 temporal partition of polyamine-associated morphology: Put marks early expansion states, Spd 114 marks transitional states, and Spm marks compact late states. These data therefore indicate that 115 polyamine identity changes where plants reside within a shared manifold rather than producing 116 separate morphologies. 117 Bridge structure reinforced this interpretation. Spd-enriched regions coincided with the narrow 118 corridors between the early hub and the late arms, including high-betweenness edges. Both 119 distal arms were enriched for Spm, but each was approached through a different transition 120 corridor. The graph therefore resolves two late rosette programs that share early history but 121 differ in the path by which they are reached. 122

Nutrition, dose, and transporter genotype alter occupancy rather than topology 123 

Nutrition modified the probability of occupying developmental regions while preserving the 124 same global topology (Figure 2A-2). The raw dataset was nearly balanced between low-MS 125 and full-MS conditions, but low-MS samples were relatively enriched in the early hub, whereas 126 full-MS samples were more frequent in distal regions. Nutrient availability therefore shifts 127 residence toward or away from late compact morphologies rather than generating a second 128 manifold. 129 Dose effects were weaker and more graded than the effect of polyamine identity (Figure 2A-130 4). Unprimed and lowest-dose samples were concentrated toward interior regions of the graph, 131 whereas higher non-zero levels appeared more often along the periphery and distal Spm-rich 132 arms. This pattern supports dose-dependent modulation of how far plants penetrate late 133 morphological states within the observed time window, although we interpret the effect as 134 secondary to priming identity and nutrition. 135 Genotype had the smallest effect on manifold geometry (Figure 2A-5). Col-0 and all five atput 136 mutants occupied the same connected structure, including the early hub, transition regions, and 137 late arms. Local enrichments were visible, with Col-0 slightly overrepresented in one distal 138 lobe, atput1/3/5 more frequent around early-to-mid connectors, and atput4 distributed broadly 139 across the graph. These differences are consistent with altered dwell times or transition 140 probabilities within shared states, rather than with genotype-specific morphotypes. 141 

Manifold straightening reveals an early steering window 142 

To determine whether the Mapper arms reflected persistent branching or transient lateral 143 deviations, we straightened the graph-derived manifold into a ribbon coordinate system (Figure 144 2B-1 to B-3). A minimum-spanning-tree backbone was refined into a medial centerline; each 145 plant was then described by normalized pseudotime s and signed transverse displacement y. 146

Discussion 176 

This study reframes image-based plant phenotyping from trait extraction to developmental 177 geometry. Across 138,223 Arabidopsis rosette images, the dominant organization was a shared 178 manifold rather than a collection of treatment-specific clusters. This distinction matters because 179 phenomics often conflates two questions: what morphologies exist in the dataset and how 180 experimental factors change occupancy of those morphologies. In this experiment, the 181 accessible morphological space is broadly shared; treatment and genotype act mainly by 182 shifting residence, transition, and terminal occupancy. 183 At the biological level, the map positions polyamines as time-localized modulators of rosette 184 architecture. Put enrichment in the early hub links putrescine with early expansion states. Spd 185 enrichment in connector regions suggests a role in transition between early and late 186 configurations. Spm enrichment at both distal arms links spermine with late, compact canopy 187 architecture. These assignments represent image-derived morphodynamic signatures rather 188 than direct biochemical causality; targeted metabolite profiling, temporal rescue experiments, 189 and transporter complementation will be needed to test mechanism. 190 Nutrient effects clarify the environmental component of this model. Full-MS conditions 191 increased occupancy of distal regions, whereas low-MS conditions favored early residence. 192 This pattern aligns with the expectation that nutrient availability affects developmental 193 progression, but the topology indicates that nutritional changes alter access to shared states 194 rather than create new ones. The same logic applies to concentration: dose modulates the depth 195 of progression into late regions, especially in Spm-rich areas, but does not reorganize the 196 manifold. 197 

Atput mutant lines provide a genetic test of the shared-manifold hypothesis. If individual 198 transporters created unique morphogenetic programs, mutant lines would be expected to form 199

Local enrichments therefore more plausibly reflect altered rates of entry, 201 retention, or exit from shared states. This interpretation is consistent with functional 202 redundancy among transporter-family members and with the idea that polyamine transport 203 tunes progression rather than specifies an entirely new architecture. 204 Methodologically, the study demonstrates why geometry-aware analysis is valuable for large 205 phenotyping screens. Standard scalar traits summarize size or color but discard the arrangement 206 of samples in morphological space. Conventional projections can visualize this space but often 207 encourage cluster narratives that are sensitive to the choice of projection parameters.[13,14] 208 By combining self-supervised visual features, hyperbolic embedding, Mapper reconstruction, 209 and ribbon straightening, the workflow retains both global topology and local deviations. The 210 workflow produces not only a visualization but also measurable quantities that can be tested 211 across experiments. 212 Two aspects of the current analysis are especially useful for future work. First, early transverse 213 offset provides a compact signature of treatment steering before trajectories converge. Second, 214 distal occupancy quantifies how strongly a condition drives plants into late rosette programs 215 within a fixed time window. These metrics convert a high-dimensional image manifold into 216 scalar phenotypes while preserving the context of the manifold from which they were derived. 217 Several limitations constrain interpretation. The analysis is image-based and therefore cannot, 218 by itself, identify the molecular state underlying a node. Area-derived pseudotime is an 219 auxiliary descriptor, not a replacement for chronological age or lineage-resolved growth rate. 220 Mapper topology depends on embedding quality, cover design, overlap, and clustering 221 parameters, and metadata-informed hyperparameter selection requires transparent reporting. 222 Because Mapper cover sets overlap, node-level enrichments are best interpreted as occupancy 223 tendencies rather than mutually exclusive states. 224

Plant material and experimental setup 234 

Arabidopsis thaliana ecotype Columbia-0 (Col-0) and T-DNA insertion lines for members of 235 the Arabidopsis polyamine uptake transporter family [atput1-1 (GABI_890c10), atput2-1 236 (SALK_119707c), atput3-1 (SALK_206472c), atput4-1 (SAIL_1275_c06), and atput5-1 237 (SALK_007135)] were obtained from the Salk Institute Genomic Analysis Laboratory 238 (signal.salk.edu/cgi-bin/tdnaexpress). Data preprocessing 257 

Arabidopsis thaliana seedlings were imaged with top-view RGB cameras in 48-well plates. 258 Plate-level images were processed by a custom detection and cropping workflow, adapted from 259 previous high-throughput plate-phenotyping work, to isolate individual rosettes (Figure 260 S3).[25] A trained object-detection network localized plant centers; detections were paired with 261 reference grid positions via Hungarian assignment; and non-maximum suppression removed 262 redundant bounding boxes. The final dataset contained 138,223 single-plant images annotated 263 with imaging round, plant index, genotype, priming identity, concentration, and medium. 264 We upscaled the cropped images fourfold using a super-resolution model prior to feature 265 extraction (Figure S4).[26] This step reduced pixelation and improved leaf-edge contrast while 266 preserving the original morphology. We excluded images that could not be confidently matched 267 to a grid position before embedding. 268 We used metadata for downstream aggregation and interpretation, not to define the initial visual 269 embedding. For graph-level summaries, we interpreted overlapping Mapper memberships as 270 node occupancy rather than as mutually exclusive sample classes. 271

Logic-Driven Clustering in Curved Space 345 

After assigning embeddings to hyperbolic intervals, we grouped plants within each interval 346 using a logic-driven clustering framework. Because standard Euclidean clustering can distort 347 distances in curved embeddings, we used a Tsetlin-machine-inspired procedure that 348 represented samples through binary, curvature-aware distance codes and refined assignments 349 by silhouette-guided feedback.[28] 350 For Euclidean features, we min-max normalized inputs and discretized them by threshold 351 encoding. For hyperbolic embeddings, we computed distances from each point to five fixed 352 landmarks in the Poincaré disk: one at the origin and four at a common boundary radius. We 353 then thresholded these distances to form binary vectors. 354 Each Tsetlin clause comprised automata controlling the inclusion or exclusion of binary literals. 355 Clauses voted for candidate cluster membership, and the procedure assigned each sample to 356 the cluster that received the strongest positive vote after silhouette-guided refinement. 357 We optimized cluster assignment using an epsilon-greedy multi-armed bandit procedure.[29] 358 The optimizer accepted proposed reassignments when they improved local silhouette beyond 359 a predefined tolerance; otherwise, it reverted the assignments. We monitored global silhouette 360 after each epoch and stopped optimization when improvement plateaued. 361 During bandit optimization, clauses that improved local silhouette were reinforced, whereas 362 clauses that reduced cohesion were weakened. This feedback updated the logical representation 363 while preserving the geometry-aware distance encoding. 364 The final clusters emerged from rule-based voting combined with geometry-aware silhouette 365 feedback. This design preserved interpretability while allowing the same framework to operate 366 on Euclidean, Poincaré, and hyperboloid representations (Figure 4). 367

Area-Derived Pseudotime 375 

To obtain an independent descriptor-based developmental score, we reused the trained visual 376 backbone with a lightweight segmentation head. We fine-tuned the head on 3,500 manually 377 annotated rosette masks and applied it to all images. For each plant, we defined rosette area as 378 the number of foreground pixels in the largest rosette component after debris removal. 379 We computed area-derived pseudotime from the log-transformed foreground area of each plant. 380 We ranked rosettes by area from smallest to largest and linearly scaled the ranks to a 0-1 381 interval, with values near 0 representing the smallest rosettes and values near 1 representing 382 the largest rosettes. We obtained early, mid, and late summaries by splitting this global 383 pseudotime distribution into tertiles (Figure S1). 384 

Multiobjective Hyperparameter Optimization 385 

We selected hyperparameters with a multiobjective NSGA-II sampler that balanced local 386 clustering quality and global topological coherence.[30] We checked local partitions with 387 silhouette-type cohesion and Davies-Bouldin separation diagnostics.[31] Because the objective 388 functions included metadata, we interpret this stage as metadata-informed model selection 389 rather than as a fully unsupervised training step. We subsequently interpreted the selected graph 390 by projecting time, line, concentration, nutrition, and priming metadata onto the fixed topology. 391 We selected the final model after 100 optimization trials. The hyperbolic cover used an overlap 392 threshold of 0.253, a minimum interval size of 64 samples, a regular four-sided, ten-at-a-vertex 393 tiling, and four tiling layers. The logic-driven clustering stage used 312 clauses, 171 automaton 394 states, a specificity setting of 3.9, a voting threshold of 15, 300 bandit-refinement steps, a 395 negative-silhouette tolerance of 0.01, and an exploration probability of 0.3. 396 These jointly optimized parameters balanced local cohesion, global topological integrity, and 397 computational efficiency, yielding the final configuration used throughout the study. 398

Contrastive Learning Configuration 399 

For contrastive learning, we normalized the complete image set using channel-wise statistics 400 from the training data. We generated two augmented views per image using random resized 401 cropping, horizontal flipping, color jitter, and Gaussian blur. 402 Training used the Adam optimizer for the DINOv2 backbone and Riemannian Adam for the 403 Poincaré head to maintain curvature-consistent updates.[32] The head comprised Poincaré-404 linear layers, Poincaré batch normalization, and a final Poincaré-linear output layer producing 405 two-dimensional embeddings within the hyperbolic disk. 406 Optimization used an InfoNCE-type contrastive loss with temperature parameter τ, following 407 the SimCLR-style training configuration described above.[27] Training proceeded for up to 408 200 epochs and stopped early when validation performance plateaued. 409
